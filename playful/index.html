Icon System · Study 01

Playful

A drawn, textural companion language. Where the clay set is rendered and dimensional, this one is ink and paper — wobbly contours, flat fills printed slightly off-register, grain in every color. Same subjects, opposite hand.

Rules of the hand

Six rules make the set feel like one person drew it in one sitting. Learned from the clay set: one hero subject per icon, a confident lean, flat cookie-cutter leaves — then everything else goes the other way.

Drawn, not renderedNo gradients, no soft shadows. Shading is hatching, stipple, or a darker cut of the same color.
Ink firstOne warm charcoal ink. Contours at 7pt, interior details at 4.5pt, always round caps.
Off-register on purposeFills are nudged +5,+4 from the linework, like a screen print that shifted on the press.
Paper shows throughHighlights are cut-outs of the paper color, not white paint. One shared wobble-and-grain filter over everything.
Lean into itEvery subject tilts 8–30°, like it was drawn fast and happy. Nothing stands at attention.
One wink eachEvery icon gets a single flourish — a sparkle, speed ticks, steam, a drip. Never more than one.

Crayon box

Eleven crayons plus ink and paper. Each icon uses at most three crayons — a hero color, a support, and butter or blush for warmth.
