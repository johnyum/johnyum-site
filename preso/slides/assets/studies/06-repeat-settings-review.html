Multi-Host Calendar · Repeat settings

When repeat settings overlap, the last one saved quietly wins

A review of how weekly and yearly repeats behave today, why they collide, whether hosts need all of it, and a conflict model we can build.

Prepared for John Yum · October 5, 2026 · Read from the native SwiftUI build. The web prototype is a line-for-line port and behaves the same.

Verdict

Yes, we are over-tooling. The collisions are not edge cases. They come from one design choice: a repeat writes its values into each night once, at Save, and whichever repeat wrote a night last owns it. Weekly price repeats also duplicate the weekend price, and seasonal repeats duplicate seasonality.

How repeat settings work today

  • Flow. The host taps nights, opens Repeat settings, picks Week or Year, chooses settings, then names and colours it.
  • Week repeats on the weekdays of the tapped nights, across the whole two-year calendar, with no date range or end.
  • Year repeats on the exact month and day of every tapped night, so Thanksgiving week is pinned to calendar dates, not to the holiday.
  • Settings a repeat can carry: price as a percent, blocked or open, min and max nights, check-in and check-out days, and cancellation policy.
  • Saving writes values into each night. A percent becomes a fixed dollar price at Save time, so it does not follow later base or season changes, and a cancelled booking's nights are never filled in.
  • Priority: the host's own edits win. Yearly beats weekly. Between two repeats of the same kind, the one written last wins.

Do hosts need all of it

Some of it, not most of it. Hosts think in a few special dates plus their normal settings. Week times year times seven settings across many listings, with two ranks of priority, is a rules engine.

Already covered

Weekend pricing

The weekend price setting does this. A weekly price repeat duplicates it and currently doubles it.

Already covered

Seasonal pricing

Seasonality already sets high and low months. Fall foliage as a repeat stacks on top of it.

Real need

Special annual dates

Thanksgiving week, Coachella, a family Christmas blackout. Most of these move, so they should follow the holiday, not the calendar date.

Real need

Day-of-week stay rules

"Saturday check-in only" or "min 2 on weekends", usually within a season. A weekly repeat has no date range, so it can't say this today.

What to do when they overlap

The more specific repeat wins. On a night where two repeats set the same thing, the one covering fewer nights a year takes it. Thanksgiving's 7 nights beat foliage's 92, whichever was saved first. Ties go to the one created more recently.

  • Percents are always against the normal price. Thanksgiving +30% means 30% over a normal night, not over foliage.
  • Work it out when drawing, don't store it at Save. Then pausing or renaming never reshuffles anything, and stopping foliage reveals Thanksgiving underneath.
  • Host edits stay on top. Add a way to hand a night back to its repeat.
  • Tips never overwrite a repeat. Gap nights and last-minute respect a repeat's minimum and price.

Considered and set aside: stacking, because +20% and +30% becoming +56% surprises hosts; and asking replace or keep on every Save, which adds a modal to every save and creates the per-night exceptions behind today's bugs.

Recommendation

Recommended

One idea: dates that repeat every year

  • A date range that repeats yearly, with an optional weekday filter.
  • An optional holiday or event anchor so Thanksgiving week lands right each year.
  • Settings: price, availability, min nights, check-in and check-out days.
  • Drop the standalone weekly cadence, max nights and cancellation policy.
  • Specific wins, worked out when drawing.
Smaller fix

Keep today's UI, fix the behaviour

  1. Re-run all repeats in specific-first order on every change.
  2. Make the quick-actions edit update in place, like Settings.
  3. Turn the overlap line at Save back on.
  4. Stop gap nights from overwriting a repeat's minimum.
  5. Stop weekly price from stacking on the weekend premium.
