## Script changes

1. **The team "after", as a specific moment** — a designer's build in review instead of flats — said right before
   "four months ago". The single most important remaining change: everything else shows what you can make; this is the
   only thing that shows what you change in others, which is the Principal question. (role +0.5)
2. **An ending for the fee case.** "Get ahead of the backlash" is followed by a Reddit thread showing backlash, and the
   script never says what happened next. If the outcome is unknown, say so and say what you'd have measured.
3. **One chosen failure in the AI work, told as a decision.** Its pick: the retired 3D character — "I spent weeks on it
   and killed it because it entertained instead of helped." Thirty seconds. (talk +0.25, role +0.5)
4. **Answer "what did you keep?" before they ask it** — taste, system thinking, how hosts think about nights; the tools
   changed, not the judgment. The "disappeared overnight" line plus "all of this in four months" will draw it.
5. **Have the "what did you design vs what does the model do" answer ready**, and say it unprompted inside the disclosure
   sentence: the chat, the objects, the physics, the highlights and the scene logic are designed; Claude writes the words
   and the page listens for them. What breaks: the model's formatting (the table), and the page's matching when an
   answer takes an unexpected shape.
6. **The disclosure: once, one sentence, before the first question types.** A running commentary on what's live drains
   the scenes.
7. **"Then I understood."** works only if the spoken line lands exactly on the tap; a long pause makes it a cliffhanger.
8. **Tighten three ⨉ lines.** "Lacked context" answers a context problem with a motion feature — say what the context
   was, then that the odometer is how the change reads at a glance. "No recurrence" restates the ⨉ — say the rule, "the
   more specific repeat wins". "No price help" — drop "without guessing, and easy to adopt" for what the slide shows: a
   tip per night, one tap to apply, undo beside it.
9. **"Everything at once"** overclaims if any of it was sequential — say "in the same week".
10. **"The team now works that way"** — add the concrete case or cut to "two designers now ship prototypes".
11. **Transportation**: let the Trips build carry the wistfulness; cut "we'd have built something truly inventive". Drop
    "painstakingly" and "grassrooted" for "built it on my own time and showed it around".
12. **Say the hold is sped up before the clock jumps**, not after.
13. **Non-determinism**: the brisket came back with different steps on two runs. One sentence on how the cookbook handles
    it (one word a step, filled from whatever the answer is).
14. **"The prototype as the spec"** will draw "what did engineering change, and what did you get wrong?" Name one thing.
    "So many edge cases" isn't an answer.

## What a 10 would take

The two things every 10 did that this one doesn't yet: the audience could repeat the argument in one sentence afterwards,
with every section as evidence for it; and the presenter showed one failure they chose, with the reasoning. The sentence
exists here ("the answer should become the thing you need; I build it, and the team forks it") but is said in pieces,
late. The failures exist (the cancelled bet, the backlash) but are framed as things that happened to the team.

Doable in days, all together: roughly 9.5 as a talk, 9.25 for the role — the two AI cards, the team after and the fee
ending, the chosen failure, the table and scrim and collage, the designed-vs-model sentence.

Needs weeks, or didn't happen: witnessed influence (a before/after on the team's cycle time, a second designer presenting
a build); measured outcomes for the shipped calendars beyond launch-week quotes (price-tip adoption, recurrence use,
support tickets — ask Airbnb if they exist); a scene where the model's agency has consequences and the design handles the
risk — a live tool call with a visible confirmation step.

The 9-to-10 gap, plainly: for the talk, structure — every 10 could lose any one slide without the argument weakening, and
this one still has sections there because they exist (Nike, the collage, Transportation's middle). For the role,
witnessed influence — the deck proves you can make the future yourself; a 10 proves other people now make it because of
you, shown rather than said. Its advice: target a clean 9 and let the live demo and the honesty do the rest.
